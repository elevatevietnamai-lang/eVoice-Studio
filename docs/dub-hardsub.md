# On-screen (burned-in) subtitles in Dub

Many source videos — Chinese short-form video in particular — carry subtitles
burned into the picture. Dub can read them and remove them:

1. **Install subtitle engine** (once). OCR (RapidOCR / PP-OCR models) and the
   frame-reconstruction code install into their own environment under
   `<engines dir>/hardsub/.venv`; the app's own Python stays untouched.
   NVIDIA GPUs get `onnxruntime-gpu`, Windows AMD/Intel GPUs DirectML, Apple
   Silicon CoreML, everything else CPU.
2. **Subtitle area** — drag on the frame to mark the band that holds the
   subtitles. A tight band avoids picking up signs or logos.
3. **Read on-screen subtitles** replaces the transcript with the on-screen
   lines, timed to the frame. Cast/voice references from a previous
   transcription carry over by time overlap, like an `.srt` import.
4. **Remove subtitles from video** rebuilds the covered pixels:
   real background revealed in neighbouring frames (camera motion compensated,
   accepted only when the surroundings still match), then the previous clean
   frame, then LaMa (AI) or a fast classical fill for what was never visible.
   The clean video becomes the job's video for preview, burned-in subtitle
   export and every video export. **Use original video** switches back.

   Rebuild modes:

   | Mode | How it fills | Speed |
   |---|---|---|
   | STTN | median of up to 5 matching neighbour frames, classical fill for pixels never exposed | fastest, no GPU |
   | LaMa (default) | nearest matching neighbour frame, LaMa for what was never exposed | medium |
   | ProPainter | same median as STTN, then LaMa refines the whole area (65% real background / 35% LaMa) to remove ringing | slowest |

   STTN and ProPainter follow the naming of VideoSubtitleRemover (MIT). They are
   temporal-background hybrids, **not** the STTN or ProPainter networks and use none of
   their weights (the ProPainter weights are non-commercial). LaMa is downloaded on
   first use; if it is unavailable, LaMa and ProPainter fall back to the STTN fill.

API: `GET /dub/hardsub/status`, `GET /dub/hardsub/install-stream`,
`GET /dub/hardsub/frame/{job_id}?t=`, `GET /dub/hardsub/ocr-stream/{job_id}?top=&bottom=&fps=`,
`GET /dub/hardsub/clean-stream/{job_id}?method=sttn|lama|propainter` (`opencv` is the legacy name of `sttn`),
`POST /dub/hardsub/restore/{job_id}`, `GET /dub/hardsub/state/{job_id}`.

Limits: an object moving directly behind the text can leave a soft smear
(no frame ever shows what was behind it); karaoke-style or strongly curved
subtitles OCR less reliably.

## AI background music (ACE-Step)

Dub → **AI background music** replaces the source background (often copyrighted
music in re-uploaded videos) with an instrumental generated by ACE-Step 1.5,
ducked under the dubbed voice. It talks to a local ACE-Step UI server
(`ACESTEP_UI_URL`, default `http://127.0.0.1:3001`, loopback only):
`GET /dub/bgm/status`, `GET /dub/bgm/generate-stream/{job_id}?style=`,
`GET /dub/bgm/preview/{job_id}`, `GET /dub/bgm/export-stream/{job_id}?lang=&music_gain=&burn=`,
`GET /dub/bgm/download/{job_id}?file=`. The export replaces the whole
background, ambient sound included. Stretch Video timing is not supported yet.
